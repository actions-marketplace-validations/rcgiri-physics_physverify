# Code of conduct

physverify is a small project about checking physics code. People who use it or contribute to it come from different fields, countries and levels of programming experience. This page says how we treat each other in the issues, pull requests, discussions and any other project space.

It follows the [Contributor Covenant, version 2.1](https://www.contributor-covenant.org/version/2/1/code_of_conduct/), in shorter words.

## What we expect

- Be kind and patient, especially with people who are new to programming or to the physics.
- Talk about the code and the results, not the person. "This step is first order" is fine; remarks about someone's ability are not.
- Assume good faith. A report that a check is wrong is a contribution, not an attack.
- Accept that you may be wrong, and say so when you are. That includes the maintainer.
- Credit other people's work, and say when an AI assistant helped you.

## What is not acceptable

- Harassment, insults, threats, or sexual language or images.
- Comments that demean people for their gender, sexual orientation, disability, appearance, ethnicity, nationality, religion, age or level of experience.
- Publishing someone's private information (an email address, a home address) without their permission.
- Repeatedly pushing a point after you have been asked to stop.

## Reports about other people's code

physverify is often used on code written by others. When you report a bug found with it in someone else's project, be polite, show how to reproduce it, say what you are not sure about, and leave the decision to the authors. Do not name or shame a project in public before its authors have had a chance to answer.

## Reporting a problem

Write to the maintainer, Ram Chandra Giri, at rcgiri.physics@gmail.com. Reports are read only by the maintainer and kept private. If the report is about the maintainer, you can contact GitHub instead through its [report abuse form](https://github.com/contact/report-abuse).

## What may happen

The maintainer may, depending on how serious it is: ask for a change in behaviour, edit or remove comments, or block someone from the project for a time or permanently. Decisions are explained to the person concerned.

## Scope

This applies in every project space (GitHub issues, pull requests, discussions) and when someone represents the project elsewhere.
